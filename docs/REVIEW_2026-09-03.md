## Overall verdict

The harness is unusually careful (every headline number reproduces from
the parquets to four decimals [V]; pairing, filtering and rank conventions
are TRIX's), and the program has one genuinely new result: a model trained
on no real knowledge graph reaches 86 / 82 percent of ULTRA-3g (P1, 0.3593
/ 0.2795 [V]). But the SOTA claim does not hold as stated. At matched diet
(3 graphs, last checkpoint) INCITE ties TRIX on both groups (−0.003
[−0.009, +0.003] / +0.007 [−0.002, +0.018]) [V]. The 4-graph rows beat TRIX
only on ind_er (+0.017 [+0.007, +0.032]) and that margin belongs to the
diet and the decay continuation, not to the synthetic prior: L1 already
has +0.0174 [V] and MX1 adds −0.0002 on ind_er [V]. The prior's own
contribution is +0.0046 on ind_e [+0.0002, +0.0092], single seed, chosen
from 32 test-set evaluations of INCITE variants [V]. The direction
"TRIX-equivalent trunk plus a synthetic rules prior" is defensible as a
paper about what a synthetic structural prior teaches a KGFM (the 2026
literature has exactly this idea for node-level graphs and relational
databases: GraphPFN, PluRel, RDB-PFN), not as an architecture paper (the
trunk is TRIX with an exact factorization that saves no time [N: 1.09x])
and not yet as a SOTA paper. Of the queued hypotheses, the proof-guided
gate is inert by construction (a one-sided loss on a sigmoid initialized
at 6.0; both gradients through it are attenuated by σ'(6) = 0.0025 [V]),
the rule head at weight 1.0 is a fivefold dose increase of the synthetic
gradient [V], and the scenario-share knob moves the training mixture's
unseen-answer share by three points [V]. The most valuable untried lever
is visible in the existing dumps: a per-query switch between L1's
seen-answer cells and MX1's unseen-answer cells scores 0.4681 / 0.3929,
+0.0076 / +0.0079 over MX1 [V], larger than anything tested so far.

## 2. Critique of the key results

* Every headline number and interval in the result notes reproduces [V].
* L1 vs MX1: same start, same schedule, but MX1 takes about 3,000 fewer
  real-graph steps (a synthetic step does not advance the real draw) [V].
  Plain continuation drifts toward seen answers (L1 vs 4g-20k: UQUA
  −0.043 / −0.044 [V]); MX1's UQUA exceeds the 20k start, so the synthetic
  steps actively lift the cell, but the seen-answer cost is confounded.
* Losses (means of the logged rows) [V]: L1 real 0.404 / 0.295 / 0.459 /
  0.284 (FB15k237 / WN18RR / CoDExMedium / NELL995); MX1 synthetic 0.143,
  real 0.397 / 0.312 / 0.468 / 0.307; MX2 synthetic 0.267, real 0.396 /
  0.311 / 0.470 / 0.300. The mix raises the real-task loss on WN18RR and
  NELL995 by 0.02.
* L1, MX1 and MX2 restart AdamW at 20k with a warmup; R1 will not; no
  from-scratch control without the mix existed.
* The 4g run's DEV10 selection was still rising at 20k [V]; "parity at 20
  percent of TRIX's steps" is also "stopped early".
* Report ind_er with and without Metafam; FLOCK's ind_er is over 22 graphs.

## 3. The MX2 failure

The note's explanation is half right. Three mechanisms are missing: (1)
gradient conflict versus dose is measurable by the cosine between a
real-step and a synthetic-step gradient at the 20k checkpoint, and one
run at half the synthetic weight separates them; (2) `_draw_from` sampled
with replacement when the pool was short, so a repeated candidate's
self-adversarial weight was multiplied [V]; (3) "certified" means not
derivable by the bounded chainer, and candidates one or two hops from the
head are exactly where a deeper chain would derive more, so the hard
negatives concentrate the chainer's truncation errors. The isolation of
relations (about 1,232 relation ids with about 20 facts each, a regime no
real graph has) is the strongest single explanation of the ind_er −0.013.

## 4. The queued hypotheses (plan v12)

* PG2: the gate is initialized at bias 6.0 and divided by σ(6): exactly
  the identity, every gradient through it multiplied by σ'(6) ≈ 0.0025,
  and nothing asks it to close. Expected: gates stay open, PG2 equals MX1
  within noise. The pruning measurement kept every tied edge and never
  recorded the realized kept fraction, so a flat curve could mean
  "nothing was pruned". Log the realized fraction and run a
  random-pruning control before any sparse-propagation work.
* RR2 at weight 1.0: BCE about 0.69 against a synthetic task loss of
  0.14, roughly 5x MX1's synthetic gradient. If run: weight 0.1-0.2,
  paired against MX1 as well.
* MXS2 at 0.37: the training mixture moves from about 0.21 to 0.18
  unseen-answer, inside noise. The informative point is 0.8-1.0.
* MX15: keep. R1: add R0 (no mix); the 30 percent mix through the
  constant phase is a threefold dose nobody validated. X1/X2: move up.

## 5. Three directions not tried, ranked

1. **A scenario-conditioned readout.** Both scenario indicators are
   observable per candidate at inference; feed them to the score head so
   the two candidate populations are calibrated separately. Upper bound
   from the dumps: a per-query oracle taking seen-answer cells from L1
   and unseen-answer cells from MX1 scores 0.4681 / 0.3929, +0.0076 /
   +0.0079 over MX1 [V]; with TRIX's seen-answer cells +0.002 / −0.008, so
   the headroom is recoverable within one trunk. Expect a third to a half
   of the bound. Cost: one continuation.
2. **The prior as the pretraining stage, with a scaling curve.** P1's
   DEV10 trace is flat after 6k steps at constant lr [V]; whether the
   plateau is optimization or the prior's size is cheap to settle
   (synthetic-only 30k and 60k with decay, dim 64, larger instances),
   then a curriculum with a low-dose mix only in the decay. This gives the
   paper its headline and its positioning against GraphPFN / RDB-PFN.
3. **No-path supervision for the unreachable bucket.** 17 percent of
   ind_e answers lie beyond six hops and score below 0.01; HM graphs are
   73-83 percent unreachable. The generator can manufacture disconnected
   answers with certain labels; mix at 5-10 percent with the unary
   channel. Expected +0.004 on ind_e if HM reaches KG-ICL's level.

Two cheap items: a gradient-cosine diagnostic between real and synthetic
batches; prior portability (mix the same union batches into ULTRA's or
TRIX's own pretraining loop).

## 6. What to stop or cut, and the minimum experiment set

Cut: MXS2 as configured, RR2 at weight 1.0, MX2b, the sparse-propagation
week unless the corrected curve beats random, E4/E5 unless scenario-aware,
the automatic test-set winner pick. Minimum set: (1) a dev suite disjoint
from the 41 test graphs for every lever decision, the recipe frozen, then
the 41 graphs once per seed, the 32 prior test evaluations disclosed; (2)
R0 and R1 from scratch at seeds 1024, 1337 and 7; (3) one matched-diet
comparison (TRIX at 30k on the 4-graph diet, or the 3-graph floor
continued with the mix); (4) X1/X2; (5) the mechanism figure (MX15, a
higher dose, the per-scenario tables, the masking trajectory, the P1
scaling points); (6) the scenario-conditioned readout before the seeds if
it clears the dev suite.

What the program can honestly claim today: a TRIX-equivalent trunk at
TRIX's zero-shot level at 30 percent of its steps (pending X1); a
synthetic rules prior that trains a KGFM to 86 / 82 percent of ULTRA-3g
with no real data; mixing it in lifts the both-unseen scenario by +0.065
on both groups at a seen-answer cost, net +0.005 on ind_e; and a 4-graph
diet plus decay worth +0.017 ind_er over the released TRIX, diet-caveated.
Not yet: a new SOTA KGFM.
